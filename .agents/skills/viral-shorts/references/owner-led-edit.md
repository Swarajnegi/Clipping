# Owner-led editing for thought.palace

Follow these stages in order for new channel edits. Keep one decision record
per clip in `Working/<project-id>/EDIT-DECISIONS.md`, or use a clearly labelled
section per clip in the existing project notes. Record the revision, choices,
and the owner's actual decision at each stop. Read that record when resuming
in a new chat. An approval for one clip or revision applies to that material.
Record decisions already supplied explicitly in the brief and resume from the
first missing one.
The owner can explicitly change the workflow for a specific brief.

## 1. Gather source clips and wait for approval

Show playable source-only proofs with original sound, exact source links and
source ranges (including all segments if the speech is tightened). In the
notes, describe what the viewer hears and sees in each of the first three
seconds. Choose fluent, energetic delivery at natural speed, with a clear
setup, development and a payoff that answers the opening question or fulfils
its premise. Keep genuine breaths and pauses where they carry meaning. Record
actual listening coverage. Stop here until the owner approves the passage.

## 2. Send the B-roll brief and wait for shot choices

Start with three numbers for each approved passage. For example, **5 B-roll
shots planned · 3 covered by playable library options · 2 new excerpts to
find**. Derive the shot count from the speech, the time the actions need to
read and the transition windows. Use music or a tempo reference already
supplied by the owner if available. While the song choice is pending, give a
provisional count and call out the slots that could change with the beat grid.

Before sourcing, map each spoken beat to a shot, speaker hold or graphic.
For a supplied reference, step through both sides of every shot boundary,
including short inserts that sparse contact sheets can miss. Compare the
reference map and the planned edit map to catch unintended omissions.

Give each shot a stable slot ID. Use a compact table with these fields.

| Slot | Spoken cue and approximate edit window | Visible action and framing | Usable duration | Playable option and source in/out, or sourcing request |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B01 | “Build the skills” on the practice beat | See the drummer repeat a stroke. Keep hands and kit in frame. | Allow a complete strike and reset. | Offer a library link and source timecode, or ask for that action. |
| B02 | “Come back your best self” on the recovery beat | Someone begins bent over and visibly straightens. Frame enough of the body to read both states. | Allow the whole movement. | Offer a library link and source timecode, or ask for that action. |

These are example actions from the [two WhatsApp references](transitions.md#the-two-11-november-edits).
The local frame breakdown in
`Main/shorts-clips/Working/reference-analysis/dialogue-cuts-20261111/FRAME-BREAKDOWN.md`
records the practice run at 16.77–19.40s and the recovery shot at 13.17–15.00s.
For actual briefs, give duration in seconds, exact spoken words and enough
detail for the owner to recognise a match. Include the visual turn or reaction
the audience should understand. Distinguish the proposed screen time from
the source duration available to trim. When asking the owner to find a new
excerpt, request around 5–10 seconds with the action and room around it.
Show a crop preview where the crop determines
whether the action reads. Search the [whole eligible library](movie-cutaways.md#choose-from-the-library)
first and show playable excerpts with their catalogue IDs or source links.

Count actual film/TV placements separately from distinct new source excerpts.
If one excerpt covers two distinct angles or actions, name both slot IDs
under one request. For example, five placements with three library slots and
one new excerpt covering the other two means **1 new excerpt to find**.
If the library covers every slot, report **0 new excerpts**. Record the shots,
offsets and holds the owner already chose. Ask only about unresolved choices.

## 3. Confirm YouTube Sound and reconcile the shot count

Use this stage for edits timed to a song. When the owner will add music
independently or requests no music, record that and proceed on dialogue timing.
Include song suggestions in that case only if requested.

Offer two or three ranked recordings with artist, exact version, excerpt
start/end and the start offset in the edit. Verify the YouTube Sound version
when it is accessible. Label a reference recording with unverified picker
availability as such. Ask for the exact picker version, excerpt or timing
reference if needed. The owner confirms the primary recording and section.

Use the actual chosen audio to mark beats that affect the edit, including
relevant downbeats, pauses and melodic changes. Log recording time,
corresponding edit time and native frame, the spoken cue and the picture change
or a marker reserved for a visual treatment to be decided in stage 5.
Confirm the final B-roll count against the grid. If the timing requires an
added slot or a materially different shot,
send the updated count and the exact sourcing request and wait for the
owner's choice for the affected slot. If the audio cannot be checked, record
the sync as pending and request the timing reference before locking the edit.

## 4. Lock the picture and transitions

Build the caption-free edit using a dialogue-matched B-roll entrance when the
spoken cue supports it, or a genuine source angle change or small visual accent
when the action or speaker needs to hold. Mark beats still needing a visual
change for the final caption stage; do not design, compose, time or render
captions yet. Give every B-roll entrance
an intentional transition, including a hard cut. Ordinary cuts and caption
changes can land closer together than paired effect presets, which retain
their existing spacing. Use a shutter, exposure pulse or another cleared
effect when it reinforces the action or verbal turn. Align SFX with their
visible cues and leave the speech intelligible. Follow [the transition guide](transitions.md).

Review the whole sequence with the approved speech, inserts and any chosen
timing track. Confirm cut cues by ear when possible. Transcript word times
are estimates until checked. Recheck the active speaker, faces and action
in the actual crop at every angle change. Record actual playback or listening coverage and any unverified
timing. A material source passage change reopens its approval and the affected
shot and song decisions. A different recording or offset reopens song approval
and timing checks. A changed shot returns to the owner for that slot. Retain
prior decisions that still apply.

## 5. Create captions last, verify and deliver

Only after the source speech, B-roll, any chosen song timing, picture cuts,
transitions and SFX are decided and the picture is locked, use
[editorial captions](editorial-captions.md) to design, place, time and render
the captions. Use the beat map and reserved markers to create the remaining
visual changes in sync with the spoken words. Check the first three seconds,
every completed caption group, both sides of each cut, transition accents,
mask overlaps and the payoff at phone size and in motion. Fix visible defects
before handing off a review. When the owner asks to see the frames before
rendering, give them the requested stills or a complete numbered frame viewer
and wait for approval before encoding the MP4. Verify
each mapped change lands on the nearest native frame or has a documented lead
or delay. Use emphasis on an already visible word when a beat falls between
word onsets. Review the intended song against the picture and SFX where the
exact timing track is available. Apply the existing decode, framing, native FPS, rights and audio
checks. Deliver the music-free master with dialogue and intentional SFX, its
cover and any confirmed YouTube Sound version, excerpt start/end and offset. Keep any
authorized timing audition in `Working`. Preserve existing resource admission
and one-heavy-stage limits.
